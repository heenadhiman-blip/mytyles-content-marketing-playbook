01 / Foundation

What Is a Marketing Funnel?

A marketing funnel explains the journey someone may take from first recognising a need to choosing a product or service. It helps content writers understand what information readers need at different points in that journey.

For MyTyles, the journey might begin when someone searches for bathroom design inspiration. Later, they may compare ceramic and porcelain tiles, check suitable finishes, and look at prices. Eventually, they may shortlist products or decide to visit the MyTyles Experience Centre.

These decisions do not always happen in a straight line. Someone might compare products, return to researching designs, and then continue towards a purchase.

TOFU · AwarenessI'm learning about my options.
↓
MOFU · ConsiderationI'm comparing what could work.
↓
BOFU · DecisionI'm ready to shortlist or act.
Remember: The funnel is a planning model, not a fixed route that every reader follows. People can enter at any stage and move backwards or forwards.
02 / The three stages

Understanding TOFU, MOFU, and BOFU

TOFU · TOP OF FUNNEL

Awareness

The reader is discovering ideas, identifying a problem, or learning the basics. They may not know which tile type they need.

Example: How to Choose Tiles When Your Room Has Almost No Natural Light

Writer's goal: Explain, educate, and build understanding without pushing a purchase.

MOFU · MIDDLE OF FUNNEL

Consideration

The reader is comparing designs, materials, sizes, finishes, applications, or installation considerations.

Example: Glossy vs Matt Tiles: Which Finish Should You Consider?

Writer's goal: Explain meaningful differences and help readers narrow their options.

BOFU · BOTTOM OF FUNNEL

Decision

The reader is closer to shortlisting a specific product, checking availability, comparing prices, or making an enquiry.

Example: A MyTyles Bathroom Wall Tiles category page with product details

Writer's goal: Support action with accurate details and a clear next step.

04 / Search behaviour

The Four Types of Search Intent

Search intent describes what someone is trying to achieve when they type a query into a search engine.

i

Informational

“I want to know.” The reader wants an answer, explanation, or tutorial.

how to choose tiles for a small bathroom

Useful pages: Guides, explainers, and FAQs.

↔

Commercial

“I want to compare.” The reader is evaluating options before a decision.

ceramic vs porcelain tiles for bathroom

Useful pages: Comparisons and selection guides.

→

Transactional

“I want to act.” The reader wants to buy, enquire, or take another action.

buy bathroom wall tiles online

Useful pages: Product, category, and enquiry pages.

⌖

Navigational

“I want to reach a place.” The reader is looking for a particular brand or page.

MyTyles website

Useful pages: Homepage and branded destinations.

05 / The connection

How Search Intent Connects to Funnel Stages

Search intent and funnel stage are related, but they are not identical. Search intent describes the immediate task. The funnel describes the reader's broader readiness to choose or act.

Search intentOften associated withWhy the mapping isn't fixed
InformationalTOFUDetailed selection questions may also be MOFU.
CommercialMOFUProduct-specific comparisons may be close to BOFU.
TransactionalBOFUThe action may be an enquiry, not necessarily a purchase.
NavigationalAny stageA search for MyTyles does not prove someone is ready to buy.
Writer's rule: Evaluate the query, reader's likely need, and the actual page content together. Do not classify a page using one keyword alone.

How to classify a new article

  1. Identify the reader's main question.
  2. Assess what they likely know already.
  3. Ask whether the content introduces, compares, or supports a decision.
  4. Choose a primary stage and, if useful, a secondary stage.
  5. Choose a helpful next step and relevant internal links.
Do not force equal numbers across stages. Many blogs naturally serve TOFU or MOFU, while category and product pages often support BOFU.
07 / Applying it to copywriting

How Funnel Knowledge Changes the Way We Write

Consider the same broad topic, bathroom tiles, written for three different reader needs.

TOFU / EDUCATE

How to Choose Tiles for a Small Bathroom

Choosing tiles for a small bathroom involves more than selecting a colour. Tile size, layout, grout joints, lighting, and surface finish can all affect how the space looks. Before comparing designs, it helps to understand how these details work together.

Approach: Explain the factors without assuming the reader has already selected a material.

CTA: Read a related guide on bathroom tile finishes.

MOFU / COMPARE

Ceramic vs Porcelain Tiles for Bathrooms

Ceramic and porcelain tiles are both considered for bathroom applications, but their properties and specifications can differ. Compare intended use, water absorption, finish, sizes, and installation requirements before narrowing the choice.

Approach: Explain trade-offs without claiming one material is universally better.

CTA: View relevant ceramic and porcelain bathroom tile options.

BOFU / DECIDE

Bathroom Wall Tiles at MyTyles

MyTyles offers bathroom wall tile options across different colours, designs, sizes, materials, and finishes. Check individual product details to compare suitable options for basin walls, shower areas, and other applications.

Approach: Make product information and next steps easy to find.

CTA: View bathroom wall tiles at MyTyles.

The difference is not only the CTA. A TOFU article should not read like a product listing; a MOFU comparison should explain real differences; and a BOFU page should not hide essential product information under lengthy background text.
08 / Connecting content

Internal Linking: Helping Readers Move Through the Funnel

Relevant internal links help readers continue learning and evaluating without starting another search. A typical journey might look like this:

TOFU · Educational guide
How to Choose Bathroom Tiles for a Small Space
↓ Relevant contextual link
MOFU · Comparison guide
Ceramic vs Porcelain Bathroom Tiles
↓ Link to suitable options
BOFU · Category page
Bathroom Wall Tiles at MyTyles

This is an illustrative pathway. A BOFU page can also link backwards to an educational guide when the reader needs help understanding a specification. Avoid placing unrelated links merely to force a conversion path.

12 / The takeaway

Write for the Reader's Next Question

Understanding the marketing funnel helps MyTyles writers move beyond simply producing content around keywords. It helps us decide who we're writing for, what information matters, how much detail to include, which pages to link, and what action to suggest next.

The goal is not to push every reader towards a purchase immediately. It is to offer useful information throughout their tile-selection journey and make the next step easier when they are ready.

One principle to remember: The funnel stage depends on the reader's needs and readiness, not simply whether the content is a blog, category page, or product page.
